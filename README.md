# CPI-Inflation-Casestudy – Excel Data Analysis Case Study

## 📌 Project Overview
This project analyzes Consumer Price Index (CPI) inflation trends across **Rural**, **Urban**, and **Combined (Rural + Urban)** sectors. Using Excel, I evaluated category-wise CPI contributions, tracked Month-over-Month (M-o-M) food inflation volatility (June 2022 – May 2023), and measured Year-over-Year (Y-o-Y) inflation shifts before, during, and after the COVID-19 pandemic (2017–2022).

## 🛠️ Excel Tools & Techniques Used
* **Data Cleaning:** Removed duplicates, handled missing values, and standardized formats using `TRIM`, `TEXT`, and Find & Replace.
* **Data Aggregation & Transformation:** Cleaned multi-sector CPI records and structured monthly and annual time-series tables.
* **Formulas & Calculations:** Computed Month-over-Month (M-o-M) percentage changes and Year-over-Year (Y-o-Y) growth rates across Rural, Urban, and Combined (R+U) sectors.
* **Data Visualization:** Designed a multi-chart executive dashboard featuring clustered column charts to compare regional disparities and seasonal inflation swings.


## 🔍 Key Insights

### 1. Category-Wise CPI Contribution
* **Food Dominates Household Inflation:** Based on the latest month's data, the **Food category is the largest contributor to CPI** (~2,200+ index points across Rural, Urban, and R+U), outweighing the next largest categories (**Miscellaneous**, **Clothing**, and **Housing**) by nearly 4x.
* **Urban vs. Rural Housing:** Housing costs contribute noticeably more to **Urban** and **R+U** inflation compared to the **Rural** sector, while Food remains consistently high across all regions.

### 2. Month-over-Month (M-o-M) Food Inflation (Jun 2022 – May 2023)
* **Seasonal Deflationary Dip (Nov 2022 – Jan 2023):** Food inflation turned negative during winter, with sharp M-o-M drops in **November 2022**, **December 2022**, and **January 2023** across all sectors.
* **Regional Peak & Trough Variations:**
  * **Rural:** Highest M-o-M food inflation occurred in **September 2022** (~0.75%), and the sharpest drop occurred in **January 2023** (~ -0.80%).
  * **Rural + Urban (R+U):** Peaked in **April 2023** (~0.75%) and hit its lowest point in **January 2023** (~ -0.60%).
  * **Urban:** Peaked in **April 2023** (~0.75%) and bottomed out early in **November 2022** (~ -0.70%).
* **Spring Rebound:** After a flat February 2023, food inflation rebounded sharply from **March to May 2023**, climbing back above +0.70% across all regions.

### 3. Year-over-Year (Y-o-Y) Trends & COVID-19 Impact (2017–2022)
* **2019 Inflation Spike:** For the combined **Rural + Urban** region, the highest overall Y-o-Y inflation rate occurred in **2019** (~8.1%), surging sharply from a 2018 low (~2.3%) before stabilizing around **5.7%–6.0% between 2020 and 2022**.
* **Pandemic Disruption on Essential Factors:** Both **Rural** and **Urban** Y-o-Y breakdown charts show heightened volatility across essential categories between **2019 and 2021** during the COVID-19 period, followed by a noticeable moderation across all series in **2022**.

## 💡 Policy & Economic Recommendations
1. **Stabilize Food Supply Chains Ahead of Spring:** Because food drives the overwhelming majority of CPI and consistently rebounds sharply between **March and May**, policymakers and supply-chain planners should build buffer stocks in Q1 to soften spring price surges.
2. **Region-Specific Inflation Interventions:** Since Urban food deflation hits earlier (November) while Rural deflation lags into January—and Urban CPI carries heavier Housing weight—targeted regional subsidies are more effective than uniform national measures.
3. **Monitor Essential Categories Post-Shock:** Maintain close tracking of essential goods (Food, Health, Housing) during supply disruptions, as COVID-19 data demonstrates how quickly volatility spreads across both rural and urban households.
